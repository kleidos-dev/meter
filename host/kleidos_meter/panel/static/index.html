The line is the mean of each pixel column, or with smoothing the moving average over the window chosen; the faint band is each column's minimum to maximum. Smoothing changes only the drawing, never the samples recorded, the statistics or a run's exports, and the live chart and a run's history each remember their own choice in this browser.

View as PNG and View as CSV save what the chart shows now, made in this page: the image with its caption, the time window, the source (live or the run) and the instrument, and the table of its pixel columns, each with the line, the band's minimum and maximum and the samples the line averages, its header naming the filter and the window.           Last change

          Which relay to fit as Relay 2

          Relay 2 closes across the shunt and Relay 1's contact, where there are only about 10 mV and little current: a dry circuit. Fit a solid-state relay (a PhotoMOS), such as the MikroE Relay 2 Click with its LCA717, and not an electromechanical one such as the HW-316: its silver contacts do not close at that voltage, and ROUTe:NULLpath ON then answers bypass check failed.

          Open, a solid-state relay leaks beside the shunt. The LCA717 leaks 1 µA at most with 30 V across it, at 25 °C (IXYS, LCA717 data sheet DS-LCA717-R04, page 2, "Off-State Leakage Current"); above 25 °C the data sheet gives only a typical curve (DS-LCA717-R04, page 4). Read as an ohmic resistance, that is 30 V / 1 µA, at least 30 MΩ (derived from DS-LCA717-R04, page 2) across a branch of about 0.1 Ω, the shunt and Relay 1's contact, which sees 1 µV at 10 µA and 164 mV at the INA228's full scale: the open relay then takes 33 fA (3.3 × 10⁻⁸ µA) and 5.5 nA, 3.3 × 10⁻⁹ of either reading. The data sheet states no figure below 30 V: held to its 1 µA at any voltage, the worst case is 1 µA, a tenth of a 10 µA reading (DS-LCA717-R04, page 2). The open relay's leakage has not been measured on this rig, nor Relay 1's contact on its own.

          Noise reduction in the INA228 (changes the measurement)

          Off on every profile, at every boot and after a reset, unless you turn it on here. On, the INA228 averages the count of conversions into each sample and a conversion takes the time chosen, so samples come less often and a short event is spread over one: the recorded samples change, unlike with the charts' smoothing. The chart, the status bar, the run's list entry, its files and the exports of a view then say so, and serve puts every profile back on the way out.

          The instrument keeps one for each profile and answers the live profile's: Set selects the profile first, which resets its channels.

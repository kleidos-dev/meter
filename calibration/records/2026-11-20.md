## 1. The Relay 2 Click as the shunt bypass

The bypass moved from the HW-316's K3 to the MikroE Relay 2 Click (`../../docs/relay2-click-wiring.md`): the LCA717 on the Click's **RL2** terminals, its control input on GPIO15, active high. Two wiring faults were found and fixed before anything below: the GPIO15 wire first sat on the wrong control pin of the Click, and K1's GPIO7 wire was disconnected, so the first `ROUTe:NULLpath ON` closed the bypass but not the lead:

```text
ERR 'ROUT:NULL ON': 104,"Lead check failed;lead check failed (the shunt still conducts: vs_pv=9000000000,1891250000,1833437500; released to path=shunt)"
```

The firmware did what it must: it refused, released to `path=shunt`, and the DUT stayed fed. With both wires fixed:

```text
ROUT:STAT?  k1=lead:closed k2=bypass:open k3=spare:off k4=spare:off path=shunt check=- pads=ok operate_settle_ms=20 release_settle_ms=50 hold_s=- hold_released=0 bypass_part=relay2click
ROUT:RES?   k1=lead:open k2=bypass:closed k3=spare:off k4=spare:off path=bypass check=ok moved=k2,k1 settle_us=71965 live=0 marked=0 vs_pv=9013750000,2294687500,4062500 hold_s=120 q=invalid
```

- `ROUTe:BYPass ON` refuses a measurement while the bypass is closed across a closed lead (`101,"Shunt bypassed"`), and `OFF` gives it back.
- The three readings of the check, with the T-Deck drawing about 88 mA: 9.014 mV across the shunt alone, 2.295 mV with the bypass closed beside it, 4.06 µV with K1 open. The second reading puts the bypass branch (the LCA717, its wires and their screws) at 0.100 Ω × 2.295 / (9.014 − 2.295) ≈ **34 mΩ**.
- The switching times of the Click (operate and release, `docs/rig-wiring.md` §8.6) were **not measured** in this session; the firmware still waits its 10 ms SSR settle.

## 2. The relay null against the hand null

`docs/rig-wiring.md` §8.6, check 3: a null taken through the relays (`ROUTe:NULLpath ON`, `CALibration:ZERO 60`) and one taken by hand (the DUT's lead opened by hand) must agree within 0.1 µV. Four 60 s nulls on range 0, `STATE`, alternated so that a steady drift does not read as a difference:

| # | Method | `offset_pv` | µV | `die_mc` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | relay | 1858853 | 1.859 | 25460 |
| 2 | hand | 1978194 | 1.978 | 26617 |
| 3 | relay | 2055547 | 2.056 | 25539 |
| 4 | hand | 2320868 | 2.321 | 25539 |

The four rise monotonically, about 0.13 µV per step: drift, not method. A least-squares fit of a constant, a linear drift and a method offset gives **hand − relay = +0.057 µV** (drift 0.135 µV per step, residuals ±0.036 µV), inside the 0.1 µV criterion. **The relay null is validated**: QA may null through the relays again.

## 3. The offset null drifts within a session

The range-0 null went from 1.86 µV (the first relay null, early in the session) to 2.22 (the `STATE` null taken before the line dropped at about 02:30), 2.94 (`SLEEP`, about 03:00), 4.38 (`SLEEP`, 03:45) and 4.61 µV (about 03:55): about 2.7 µV in two hours, most likely thermal EMF in the sense loop, warmed by the 0.16 W the reference dissipates. A null therefore belongs to its session and is never part of the calibration: the guided calibration takes its own before and after, QA brackets every capture with its own, and the registry stores the nulls of a calibration only as evidence.

## 4. The shunt, two points against the 100 Ω reference

Method: `../README.md`, "The shunt, two points". Reference: **VPG Y0785100R000T9L, 100 Ω ±0.01 %** (S102C foil: TCR ±2 ppm/°C, load life ±0.005 %), no certificate. One bench fact differs from the guide's step 4: on this rig the cell's + feeds the **VBUS** screw and a VBUS–VIN+ jumper carries it to VIN+. For the calibration the cell's + was moved to **VIN+** and the VBUS wire went to the reference's top lead, so VBUS sensed the reference and nothing else.

### Run 1 (02:49–03:30): drifting joints — superseded

The reference's leads were twisted and clipped to the pigtails. `STATE`, 2000 samples a point, the 10 s `STATE` nulls taken before the line dropped at about 02:30 (range 0 2.217 µV, range 1 2.193 µV):

|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100035634 | 26953 |
| 0 | 100046072 | 27710 |
| 1 | 100046169 | 27039 |
| 1 | 100043185 | 27867 |

Every limit of the tool held (repeats of 104 and 30 ppm against 200, ranges 38 ppm apart against 1000), and **100.043 mΩ** was stored (`R100_20261120`). Ten minutes later, on fresh `SLEEP` nulls, the same wiring read 100.153 to 100.163 mΩ on range 0 and 100.169 to 100.177 on range 1 (300 samples a point), and by 03:30 range 0 read 100.209 to 100.212 mΩ on both profiles: **100.04 → 100.21 mΩ over about 40 minutes**. A shift of 0.17 % is a contact of about 0.17 Ω in series with the reference's VBUS path, about 7 mV on the 4 V it senses: the twisted joints, not the shunt. The run is filed as `superseded`, and the lesson is in the guided calibration: the joints are screwed, and a closure gate compares the first and the last block of each range.

## 5. What the three runs say about the range split

The split was 38 ppm in run 1, 153 ppm in the `SLEEP` points at 03:00, and 562 ppm in run 3, each time with range 0 measured before range 1. Drift is confounded with range, so the split is **not yet a property of the instrument**: applying one value to both ranges, with half the split as a budget term, is the honest result today. The guided calibration measures the ranges in the order 0, 1, 1, 0, which takes a linear drift out of the split; a later calibration that shows a stable split may apply each range's own mean.

Between runs, range 0 read 100.160 mΩ (run 2) and 100.088 mΩ (run 3), 720 ppm apart, more than the budget's 112 ppm repeatability: run 2's null was 1.44 µV older, which on a 4.06 mV signal explains up to 355 ppm. Both sit inside 100.116 ± 0.085 mΩ, so the filed interval holds; the guided calibration takes its repeatability from the scatter between blocks with fresh nulls around them, not from within one block.

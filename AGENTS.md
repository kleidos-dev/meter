# AGENTS.md — Kleidos bench meter

Rules for anyone, person or agent, changing this repository. [README.md](README.md) says what the instrument is, how it is built, flashed and wired; [docs/scpi.md](docs/scpi.md) is its protocol.

## What this repository is

- **The instrument, not the measurements.** The firmware of the INA228 bench meter, its schematic and wiring, its bill of materials, its calibration procedure, records and registry, its SCPI command set and a host client. The measurements are taken by the Kleidos firmware repository's QA harness (`qa/tools/power_campaign.py`, `MeterLink`) and filed in that repository's power store (`qa/measurements/power/`); results are copied wherever they are needed from there. Never file a measurement here, and never move the power store here.
- **Public with the rest of the organization**, on the owner's word, as one commit; its private history is not carried over.
- **Licenses** (owner, 2026-11-19): the firmware, the host software and the documents are **GPL-3.0-or-later**; the hardware design files (`hardware/`: the KiCad schematic, its generator, its renders, the BOM) are **CERN-OHL-S-2.0**. Both are OSI-approved. Every file names its copyright holder and its license: two SPDX lines in its own header, the copyright (year and holder's name, no e-mail) directly above the license identifier, or, for a file that cannot hold a comment (a license text, the generated schematic and its renders, a calibration record), an explicit entry in [REUSE.toml](REUSE.toml), which has no catch-all. `reuse lint`, the quality kit's header check and `python3 scripts/check_spdx.py` (pre-commit and CI; `--fix` adds a missing copyright line) must pass. Never add a Creative Commons license to anything of ours.

## The calibration registry is the calibration

- `calibration/` holds the calibration of every instrument, versioned (`calibration/README.md`): configurations, instruments and their append-only events, records, and the generated `index.json`. What the instrument keeps in its flash (a record of the calibration record log, from FW-16; an NVS blob before) is a working copy; the Kleidos QA harness reads the registry at a pinned commit, never the instrument. A correction is a new event and a new record, never an edit: the check holds each filed record to its hash.
- `python3 scripts/check_calibration_registry.py` (pre-commit, `scripts/run_tests.sh`, CI) must pass, and `--write` regenerates `index.json` after any change to a configuration or an instrument.
- **No real instrument serial is tracked** (owner, 2026-11-27): a serial (`firmware/src/identity.h`) is a digest of the MAC, so the bench instrument is `KM-0000000000` everywhere (`tests/ci/test_instrument_serials.py`); nor are the MAC, the port or the USB serial.

## The protocol is a contract

- [docs/scpi.md](docs/scpi.md) is normative. A change to a command, a response, a report key, an error number or a stream record changes that page, the host client (`host/kleidos_meter/`), its simulator and its tests in the same commit, and is announced to the Kleidos QA harness, which speaks the protocol on its own (`MeterLink` and its fakes in `qa/tests/tools/meter_sim.py`).
- **The command table is the command set** (FW-30): `kCommands` in `firmware/src/console.cpp` is its one definition, each entry with its refusals (D, R, C) and the most parameters it takes (`takes( n )`; an entry that names no count takes none), and nothing lists the headers by hand. `scripts/scpi_table.py` reads it; `tests/firmware/test_scpi_doc_matches_table.py` holds docs/scpi.md's command reference to it header for header, and the host tests' sweep (`firmware/test/native/test_sweep.cpp`) runs every entry, so a new command needs its row on the page and its fixture in the sweep ([docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md)). The native bridge (`make -C firmware/test/native bridge`) answers it on standard input and output. **The simulator answers as the firmware does** (FW-31): `host/kleidos_meter/sim_table.py` copies the table, held to it row for row through `scripts/scpi_table.py`, and the conformance suite (`host/tests/conformance.py`, run by `scripts/run_tests.sh` on the simulator and on the bridge) requires the same keys, forms and error codes of both (of the pin map and the averaging, every byte), so a change to a report or a refusal changes the simulator in the same commit.
- Report keys keep the device's integer units in their names (`_na`, `_pv`, `_uohm`, `_mc`, `_us`). A key is never renamed or given another unit: a new meaning is a new key.
- The `TEXT` stream record (`METER t=<us> ch=<ch> v=<value>`) is the one recorded `meter.log` files hold; it does not change.
- Every refused unit queues exactly one error and changes nothing; later units of the message still run.
- **Versions** ([CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md), "The version rule"): a new command, key, error, record or observable behavior is a minor version, a fix nobody sees on the wire a patch, a removal a major; a changed meaning is not allowed. The change bumps `firmware/version.txt` and its copies (host package, simulator, host-test harness) and adds its changelog entry in the same commit, and the version is tagged `v<version>` (annotated, signed) once that commit is on `main`. `tests/firmware/test_versioning.py` holds the version, its copies and the changelog together.

## The pin map

The GPIOs of the five roles (the INA228's SDA, SCL and ALERT, Relay 1, Relay 2) are data the instrument stores and the panel changes (0.18.0; [docs/scpi.md](docs/scpi.md#the-pin-map), [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) §2.7):

- **The table is the board.** `firmware/board/pins.toml` describes the carrier pin by pin; `scripts/gen_pin_table.py` writes both copies, `firmware/src/pin_table.h` and `host/kleidos_meter/board_pins.json`, with one digest, and `--check` (pre-commit, `scripts/run_tests.sh`) holds them: never edit a copy.
- **A role or a rule changes everywhere in one commit**: `pins.toml`, `pinmap::validate()` (`firmware/src/pinmap.h`), the vectors it prints (`firmware/test/native/golden/pinmap.vectors`), its Python port (`host/kleidos_meter/pins.py`), its JavaScript one (`host/kleidos_meter/panel/static/js/pins-math.js`) and [docs/scpi.md](docs/scpi.md). A new digest makes every stored record read as none.
- **An apply proves before it saves**, and puts the last good map back with exactly one `117` when the proof fails. A relay's pad proves nothing: a map that moves one reads `verified=0` until a null path passes its check; what the DUT's current shows at rest (a lead opened or a bypass closed by a wrong active level, from 2 mA) puts the map back like a failed proof (`reason=rest`).
- **Recovery needs no I2C**: no role takes the console's UART, and `SYSTem:PINS:DEFault` needs no INA228.

## Firmware

- ESP-IDF only (`framework = espidf`), no Arduino, no third-party device library. The toolchain is pinned in `firmware/platformio.ini` and the pins and the strict warning set are guarded by `tests/firmware/test_project_pins.py`. Compiler and linker warnings are errors, as the compiler is actually told: ESP-IDF gives every component `-Wno-error=` and `-Wno-<warning>` flags that a later `-Werror` does not undo, so `build_src_flags` answers each by name, and `-Wl,--fatal-warnings` is on the link (`firmware/CMakeLists.txt`). `tests/firmware/test_effective_compile_flags.py` reads the compile database (`pio run --project-dir firmware -e meter -t compiledb`, never a verbose build) and CMake's link command; CI's firmware job runs it after the build with `METER_BUILD_REQUIRED=1`, which turns a build guard's skip into a failure.
- Build: `pio run --project-dir firmware -e meter`. On the Kleidos bench every pio command goes through that repository's `scripts/pio-job` (`scripts/pio-job run --project-dir <this repo>/firmware -e meter`), which serializes builds and uploads across checkouts. CI builds the image on every push and pull request (the `firmware` job of `.github/workflows/check.yml`), with the pinned ESP-IDF installed by `scripts/install_idf.sh`: a byte copy of the firmware repository's script, recorded in `scripts/install_idf.json` and held to it by `tests/firmware/test_install_idf_copy.py`. Never edit the copy; copy it again from that repository and update the manifest's commit and digest.
- Flash only through the CH343 port, naming it (`KLEIDOS_METER_PORT`); the upload guard refuses anything else. The meter is the bench's to flash; a Kleidos board never is, from here.
- Integer arithmetic for every value on the wire: no float in the measurement path, no locale.
- **Fixed-width integers, unsigned bit work** (FW-23, MISRA C++:2023 6.9.2 and 7.0.5): `firmware/src` names `std::int32_t`, `std::uint32_t`, `std::uint64_t` or `std::size_t`, never `int`, `unsigned`, `long` or `short` (the few a printf format's arguments needed went with the formatter, FW-26); masks and bit positions are `std::uint32_t`, flag tests and field reads go through `firmware/src/bits.h`, and a literal that meets an unsigned operand carries its `U`. `scripts/misra_register.py` and `scripts/misra_rules.py` fail anything else ([docs/misra.md](docs/misra.md), "Held", D12).
- **No formatted output and no varargs** (FW-24 to FW-26, MISRA C++:2023 21.10.1 and 30.0.1): every response, error, stream record and dump row is composed with the bounded writer of `firmware/src/text.h` (typed appends on `std::to_chars`, no format string, nothing allocated) through the console's typed members (`openUnit()`, `respondText()`, `respondNumber()`, `respondScaled()`, `openRecord()`/`endRecord()`, `openError()`/`failText()`), never `snprintf`, `<cstdio>`, `<cstdarg>` or a C ellipsis; and no array decays to a pointer (7.11.2: a buffer travels as a `std::span`, a character array as `chars::untilNul()`). `scripts/misra_rules.py` and clang-tidy's `cppcoreguidelines-pro-type-vararg`, `cert-dcl50-cpp` and `cppcoreguidelines-pro-bounds-array-to-pointer-decay` fail anything else, and the golden transcript (`firmware/test/native/golden/console.transcript`) holds every byte on the wire ([docs/misra.md](docs/misra.md), "Held", D3).
- **No dynamic memory in our code** (0.7.0): FreeRTOS objects and task stacks are created over static storage (`xQueueCreateStatic`, `xSemaphoreCreateBinaryStatic`, `xTaskCreateStaticPinnedToCore`), the console runs on ESP-IDF's main task, which `app_main()` never leaves (FW-17: so nothing frees it) and whose stack and control block are static too (the review of 2026-11-24: ESP-IDF's startup tasks are linked wrapped onto static slots), buffers are static, and there is no `new`, `delete`, C or `heap_caps` allocator, smart pointer or standard container in `firmware/src`. The claim is `allocs_since_main=0 frees_since_main=0` for every operation the SCPI table offers (FW-19). The source gate (`tests/firmware/test_no_dynamic_memory.py`), the object gate (`scripts/check_heap_objects.py`, CI), the reachability gate (`scripts/check_heap_reachability.py`, CI's firmware job, FW-19: the linked image's call graph, read with pyelftools from the relocations the device link keeps, `-Wl,--emit-relocs`, from every own function to any allocator, each ROM function reached and each edge cut listed with its reason, `tests/firmware/test_heap_reachability_gate.py`), the host tests' every-command session (`firmware/test/native/test_heap.cpp` `kEveryCommand`, both counts 0 after each message, held to every header of the command table by `tests/firmware/test_heap_session_covers_the_table.py`) and the device's own counts (`DIAGnostic:HEAP?`, `heap_monitor.h`: from FW-07 every allocation from ESP-IDF's startup on, split at `app_main()`'s first line, where from FW-17 the 0.7.0 steady state starts too, and from which the required count is zero, the boot included) hold it; what ESP-IDF still allocates, all of it before `app_main()` (1 allocation, the C library's 4-byte `environ`, and no free, from the review of 2026-11-24, which linked ESP-IDF's IPC and main tasks, its flash lock and its system interrupts wrapped onto static slots and the firmware's own interrupt router; 16 and 4 from FW-18, which took PSRAM out of the heap, linked PSA's initialization and the idle tasks' memory wrapped onto static answers in `firmware/src/startup_memory.h`, and arms the brownout detector itself in `firmware/src/brownout.h`), is listed row by row in [docs/misra.md](docs/misra.md) (D1, which `tests/bench/test_bench_support.py` holds to `heap_expectations.toml`; the maintenance operations' deviation went with the last of them in FW-16, and the register names none), held by one deny list of allocating ESP-IDF entry points, one allow-list of the calls we still make (empty since FW-16, and from FW-17 absent from the host harness, which fails any listed call not made safe) and one made-safe list of the calls the image's configuration takes the allocation out of (FW-16: the calibration log's `esp_flash_write()` and `esp_flash_erase_region()`, whose region check is linked wrapped onto a static table; and its `esp_flash_read()`, whose port refuses a destination outside internal RAM, which ESP-IDF would read through a heap bounce buffer), in three copies kept equal by `tests/firmware/test_idf_allocation_lists.py` (the source gate's, the object gate's and the host harness's ledger, `firmware/test/native/harness/idf_ledger.h`, which fails a host test whose code calls one on neither list; the allow-list only shrinks, the deny list only grows, and each made-safe call's mechanism is held in the tree); the object gate's `--elf` leg fails an image holding `operator new` or `delete`, the C++ exception runtime or NVS (FW-16), or a PSA or mbedTLS function (FW-18: PSA's startup initialization is linked wrapped onto an answer that initializes nothing). **The build holds only the firmware's component and what it requires** (`set(COMPONENTS src __pio_env)` in `firmware/CMakeLists.txt`, FW-16): a component ESP-IDF builds can be linked by another's optional dependency, as NVS was through mbedTLS's PSA storage after the firmware stopped calling it, so a new requirement goes in `firmware/src/CMakeLists.txt` with its reason, and `tests/firmware/test_meter_sdkconfig_reaches_the_build.py` fails a build that holds NVS, a radio or a retired driver. Sites are named one by one by a census build (`-DMETER_HEAP_CENSUS` through `PLATFORMIO_BUILD_SRC_FLAGS`, read by `scripts/heap_census.py`). **The bench suite** (`tests/bench`, `--run-bench`, under the bench lock; `docs/testing.md`) holds the boot's counts and each command family's to `tests/bench/heap_expectations.toml`: a change that removes an allocation lowers that file in the same commit, and nothing raises it.
- **The design is [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md)**, kept current: a change that makes a statement there false corrects it in the same commit. [docs/firmware-design.md](docs/firmware-design.md) is the design record of 0.1.0, frozen on 2026-11-22; nothing but it and the changelog cites its sections, and `tests/firmware/test_no_stale_references.py` fails on a citation anywhere else.
- **One core owns the I2C bus** (0.16.5): every transaction is made on the core that opened the bus (Core 1, the sampler's). Until FW-14 that was where ESP-IDF's I2C master had its interrupt, and a NACKed transaction from the other core left a completion latched that the next one took for its own, `ESP_OK` and zeros ([docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) §2.2); from FW-14 the bus is the own I2C master, which refuses every call from the other core before it touches a register. Code reaches the bus only through `Ina228Driver`'s transaction entries, which make the call in place there and route it through `esp_ipc_call_blocking()` from the other core, one transaction per call. The source gate (`tests/firmware/test_i2c_on_bus_core.py`) fails on a call into `i2c_bus` anywhere the routing does not own and on any call of ESP-IDF's I2C master, and the host harness fails every `Bench` case that reached I2C0's registers off the bus's core.
- **Interrupts are routed by our own code** (FW-09): the ALERT pin's interrupt goes to a free level-1 line of Core 1, and UART0's (FW-12) to one of Core 0, through `firmware/src/interrupt.cpp` (the CPU's descriptor and handler tables, the interrupt matrix, nothing allocated), and the pin's interrupt type, enable and status are set at the register (ESP-IDF's `hal/gpio_ll.h`) in `sampler.cpp` alone ([docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) §5.1). No source calls ESP-IDF's interrupt allocator or its GPIO interrupt driver; `tests/firmware/test_interrupt_routing.py` holds both, and the host harness fails a case whose level interrupt its handler leaves pending.
- **ESP-IDF drivers are replaced, not wrapped** (FW-10): where the zero-allocation work retires an allocating ESP-IDF driver, the firmware drives the peripheral through ESP-IDF's low-level layer (`hal/*_ll.h`, inline register access) with static state, polled where it can be, and the harness fakes the registers rather than the driver. The LED's RMT frame is the first ([docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) §8), and ESP-IDF's task watchdog the second: `CONFIG_ESP_TASK_WDT_EN` is off and `firmware/src/watchdog.cpp` runs Timer Group 0's MWDT itself, fed once the console, the sampler and Core 0's idle task have all checked in (FW-11, §5.1). The third is the console's UART: ESP-IDF's UART driver is gone, and `firmware/src/uart0.cpp` sets UART0 on its registers (`hal/uart_ll.h`), fills a static receive ring from an interrupt it routes itself, and writes by polling the FIFO (FW-12, §4.2). The fourth is the I2C master: `firmware/src/i2c_bus.cpp` drives I2C0 on its registers (ESP-IDF's `hal/i2c_ll.h`), each transaction polled to its end, the clock changed by rewriting the timing registers, nothing allocated (FW-13, §2.4), and the INA228's driver makes every transaction through it, a clock change included (FW-14). The fifth is NVS: the calibration is a record of the firmware's own append-only log over two 4 KiB sectors of the `calib` partition at 0x9000 (`firmware/partitions.csv`, `firmware/src/flash_log.cpp` and `flash_port.cpp`, §3.7), written through `esp_flash_*` from a static buffer with ESP-IDF's region check linked onto a static table that allows only those sectors and, from FW-03 (0.18.0), the pin map's second log of the same kind over two sectors of the `pins` partition at 0xB000 (`firmware/src/pinmap_store.h`) (`-Wl,--wrap=esp_partition_is_flash_region_writable`, `CONFIG_SPI_FLASH_DANGEROUS_WRITE_ALLOWED`), and a stored shunt outside 0.05..0.2 Ω is refused like a bad CRC, never clamped (FW-16); no migration from NVS, so the first boot of that image is `q=uncal` until `kleidos-meter calibration restore`. `scripts/check_drivers_absent.py` (CI's firmware job) fails an image whose link map holds any member of a retired component's archive (its `RETIRED` list, `nvs_flash` and `nvs_sec_provider` from FW-16); `tests/firmware/test_drivers_absent_gate.py` keeps every retired component out of the firmware's requirements and its sources' includes.
- **Serial-only, and the unused hardware parked** (0.16.6, the owner's ruling of 2026-11-21): the NW-00 radio A/B failed (`calibration/records/radio-ab-2026-11-21.md`), so the meter never links Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, the PHY or coexistence, and its USB-Serial-JTAG is off in `sdkconfig.defaults`; the panel runs on the host over the cable (`kleidos-meter serve`, "The panel" below). `scripts/check_radio_absent.py` (CI's firmware job) fails an image that defines any of their symbols. Every broken-out GPIO the firmware does not use is parked at boot, an input with the internal pull-down, as the pin map the boot runs leaves it (`pinmap::parkedSet()`, from FW-04; the default map's set is `firmware/src/relay.h` `kParkedPins`, held equal by a `static_assert`), and the rules refuse a strapping, memory, UART0, USB or LED pin to every role ([docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) §9). **No GPIO is named outside the pin map**: `main.cpp`, `relay.cpp` and `pin_boot.cpp` take every pin from it, and nothing but the map's read precedes the relays' release (`tests/firmware/test_meter_relays_fail_safe.py`).
- **The measurement's hot path runs from IRAM** (0.16.1): both cores share the flash cache, and code the sampler fetches from flash on Core 1 waits on Core 0's evictions, which with a radio lost up to 17 % of `STATE`'s conversions ([docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) §5.2). A function or constant the loop or the ALERT handler reaches, the own I2C master's transactions included, is `IRAM_ATTR` / `DRAM_ATTR` (ours), or moved by `sdkconfig.defaults` (ESP-IDF's: FreeRTOS). `scripts/check_iram_hot_path.py` (CI's firmware job) walks the call graph from the objects' relocations and fails on anything it reaches in flash; a function deliberately left there goes in its `COLD` list with the reason no conversion runs it.
- **Formatted and analyzed**: `.clang-format` is the Kleidos firmware repository's style (clang-format 23.1.5, pre-commit and CI), and `scripts/run_static_analysis.sh` runs clang-tidy (the quality kit's root `.clang-tidy` and the meter's own checks on top of it in `firmware/.clang-tidy`) over every source of `firmware/src/CMakeLists.txt` twice, as the device and as the host tests compile it, and cppcheck (`.cppcheck-suppress`) over the same sources, every finding an error. The analyzers' versions have one definition site, `scripts/analysis_toolchain.sh`; what the gate refuses to judge (exit 2) and its self-test are in [docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md#what-runs). A finding is fixed, or suppressed at its line with the reason beside it and an entry in [docs/misra.md](docs/misra.md), the register of what the firmware holds of MISRA C++:2023 and where it departs. A commit that only reformats is listed in `.git-blame-ignore-revs`.
- **Documented**: every symbol of `firmware/src` carries a Doxygen comment, private class members, file-static functions and constants, anonymous-namespace symbols and function-local classes included, and every function a `@brief`, one `@param[in|out]` per parameter and `@return` or `@retval`; `mkdir -p build && doxygen Doxyfile` (Doxygen 1.17.0, pre-commit and CI) exits 0 only with zero warnings, undocumented symbols included, and `scripts/check_doc_links.py` then fails what Doxygen does not warn on ([docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md#what-runs)). The public contract goes on the `.h` declaration and is never repeated on the `.cpp` definition, and so does a private member's, on its declaration in the class; a file-static or anonymous-namespace symbol is documented where it is defined, in its `.cpp`; each file joins its topic of `docs/groups.dox` with an `@addtogroup` wrap inside its namespace and names it with `@ingroup` in its `@file` block; `docs/firmware.dox` is the reference's front page and `docs/README.md` that of the site every Markdown page joins, linked in GitHub's form ([docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md#the-documentation-site)). The house style is the Kleidos firmware's, with no coverage baseline.
- **The firmware is tested on the host as it is built** (`docs/testing.md`): its sources compile unchanged against the fake ESP-IDF of `firmware/test/native/harness/`, on a simulated board. A change to the firmware comes with the tests that show its decisions: the report's gate refuses an MC/DC condition left uncovered without a written reason in `firmware/test/native/mcdc_exclusions.toml`.
- **One image** (0.8.0 and later): `meter`, which drives the shunt bypass on the MikroE Relay 2 Click (active high on GPIO15) and the lead on the HW-316's K1, the rig as wired (rev D). The `meter_relay2` image of 0.3.0 to 0.7.0 drove the same rig and the registry accepts both names for it (`calibration/README.md`); an image older than 0.8.0 named `meter` drove the HW-316 bypass and must not run on this rig (`docs/relay2-click-wiring.md`).
- New source files start with the same two SPDX lines the others carry (GPL-3.0-or-later; CERN-OHL-S-2.0 under `hardware/`); the version lives in `firmware/version.txt` and is bumped by the version rule above.

## Before you commit

`scripts/run_tests.sh` (every test and the report) and `pre-commit run --all-files` (every hook) are the two every change runs; [docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md) lists every gate with its command.

**One CI run per ready head**: iterate as a draft (its Check job fails on purpose), run the local gates, mark it ready when they pass, push again only if something must change. A ruleset requires Check on `main`: fast-forward a head only once its ready run made Check green ([docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md)).

## The panel

`kleidos-meter serve` (`host/kleidos_meter/panel/`) is the panel on the PC; [docs/panel.md](docs/panel.md) says how it is used and [docs/http-api.md](docs/http-api.md) what it answers. These hold whatever is changed in it:

- **The sole opener of the port.** It opens the port exclusively, every message goes through its one executor, and whoever else needs the line meanwhile comes in through the passthrough, one client at a time, byte for byte. The panel never reaches the meter any other way, and the firmware has no command, session or lock for it.
- **It never changes `FORMat`, and never sends `*RST`.** The host parses the stream in the format it finds (`TEXT`, the default); `format` is declared `writable=False`, and only a person at the console or a passthrough client changes it.
- **It hands the instrument back as it found it.** What it found is a snapshot of the settings that shape a capture, taken at the start; what it changed that differs is written back and read back on the way out and before the line is lent. A window it did not open is never closed, a sequence's cleanup always runs and leaves `path=shunt`, and no order carries `FORCe` or `LIVE` that a person did not give: a sequence that needs `FORCe` asks first. A new setting the panel writes is declared in `SETTINGS`, which is also what it restores.
- **The pin map is never restored on the way out**: it is the board's wiring, in no snapshot and not in `SETTINGS`; only an Apply, a Defaults or a passthrough client changes it.
- **Its data stays outside every repository.** Runs go to the data directory, refused inside a git work tree, and a shunt override beside it; nothing a run holds is committed here or filed anywhere but by the Kleidos harness.
- **Its SCPI is held to the firmware's table.** `host/kleidos_meter/panel/schema.py` is the one declaration of the settings, the relay orders, the sequences and the routes: the API dispatches on it, the page draws its controls from it, and a test holds every header and flag it names to `kCommands`. A route, a sequence or an argument that changes changes it, its contract (`host/tests/test_panel_contract.py`), [docs/panel.md](docs/panel.md) and, by `python3 scripts/gen_http_api_doc.py`, docs/http-api.md (never edited by hand) in the same commit: `host/tests/test_panel_doc.py` fails otherwise, and runs the page's simulator examples.
- **Unauthenticated, so on the loopback.** It binds 127.0.0.1 unless `--allow-remote` says otherwise, a GET never changes the instrument, and a change carries `X-Kleidos-Meter: 1`. No route may weaken a guard of `host/kleidos_meter/panel/guards.py`.
- **No third-party script, no build step.** The page is plain ES modules of this package, and nothing it loads is another origin's.

## The schematic

- `hardware/rig.kicad_sch` and its project files are generated by `hardware/gen_rig.py`, and `rig.pdf`, `rig.svg` and `rig-calibration.svg` (sheet 2, the shunt's calibration) by `hardware/export_rig.py` in KiCad 10.0.8's container; CI holds both to their bytes. The drawing follows the Kleidos KiCad style, owned by the brand repository and copied as `hardware/kicad/` (held to `KIT.json`): never edit the copy; change brand, run its `tools/sync_kicad.py --copy`, generate and export again. ERC allows no message; the netlist must stay `EXPECTED` in `export_rig.py` unless the wiring on the bench changes, and then both change in the same commit with a new revision in `gen_rig.py`.

## Organization rules

The rules every kleidos-dev repository shares are the [organization rules](https://github.com/kleidos-dev/.github/blob/main/quality/agents/block.md): read them before changing anything here.

## Conventions

- **[docs/README.md](docs/README.md) indexes the documents, and [docs/usage.md](docs/usage.md) is the one a user follows.** A new page in `docs/` is listed in the index in the commit that adds it (`tests/firmware/test_docs_index.py`). Every command of the usage guide was run and its output is real: the `--sim` ones are compared by `host/tests/test_usage_doc.py`, so a change to what they print changes the page in the same commit, and the panel's page likewise (`host/tests/test_panel_doc.py`). [docs/cli.md](docs/cli.md) and [docs/python-api.md](docs/python-api.md) are generated (`scripts/gen_cli_doc.py` from the parsers, `scripts/gen_python_api_doc.py` from the docstrings) and never edited by hand: a change to a subcommand, an option, an exit status (the module's `EXIT_STATUS`), a public signature or a docstring writes them again in the same commit, and `host/tests/test_cli_doc.py` and `host/tests/test_python_api_doc.py` fail otherwise. A new subcommand goes in `cli.SUBCOMMANDS` with its example in the generator, and every public name of the host package has a docstring ([host/README.md](host/README.md), "Docstrings"). The README's quick start is held the same way; its layout draws every file of `firmware/src`, every page of `docs/` and every top-level entry (`tests/firmware/test_readme.py`), and a settled question is struck with its date and the link to its record.
- English in code, comments, docs and commit messages; Markdown prose is never hard-wrapped (one line per paragraph and per list item).
- Commit messages: signed, imperative subject of at most 72 characters with no final period, a blank line, body wrapped at 72, no attribution lines: the quality kit's `kleidos-commit-msg` hook checks each commit, and its quick workflow every commit of a pull request. Never `--no-verify`, never force-push.
- **A skip in CI names where the test runs instead** (FW-31, `SKIPS` in `scripts/test_report.py`, `tests/ci/test_skips.py`): any other skip fails the report's gate, and what the run itself built never skips (`METER_BRIDGE_REQUIRED`, `METER_BUILD_REQUIRED`, `METER_BROWSER_REQUIRED`). Every module of the host package has a branch floor in `host/branch_floors.toml` that only rises (`tests/ci/test_host_floors.py`); a new module gets its floor in the commit that adds it. The page's pure modules have theirs in `host/js_floors.toml` (CI's panel job). Every mutant is killed: the host's (`host/mutants/`) in every run, the firmware's (`scripts/mutants/`) by hand and before a release; new code brings its own ([docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md#mutants)).
- CI follows the organization's CI convention (job names `"<emoji> <description>"`, stages cheapest first through `needs:`, path filters in the What changed job, under which a change to any script, test or Doxygen input a filtered job reads runs that job (`tests/ci/test_changes_filter.py`), a superseded run cancelled, every action pinned to a commit); `tests/ci/test_workflow_convention.py` holds `check.yml` and the advisory `codeql.yml` and `security.yml` to it.
- **The Kleidos quality kit runs here by name** (kleidos-dev/.github, `@v3`; owner, 2026-11-24): `check.yml` calls its `quick.yml` (the policy checks, every pre-commit hook on every file, reuse and the header lines, gitleaks over the history, the commit messages) and its `check.yml` (mypy strict in the three groups of `pyproject.toml`, OSV-Scanner), and ends with the Check job, the required check, which the kit holds to its model; nothing of the kit is held but its base configurations (`.github/quality/SOURCE.json`) and the waivers (`.github/quality/waivers.toml`, each weakening with its reason). Its hooks and the canonical third-party hooks are the first of `.pre-commit-config.yaml`, at the kit's release; the quick job skips the ones this repository runs in jobs of its own, and `tests/ci/test_lint_gates.py` names the job that runs each. The spelling locale is `en-us`; a word goes into `.typos.toml` only with the reason it is not a typo.
- Private bench data (port names, USB serials, MACs, host names) never enters a tracked file; gitleaks (`.gitleaks.toml`, pre-commit and CI) fails a port name with a USB serial in it, a USB serial number as macOS prints it, and a MAC address.
